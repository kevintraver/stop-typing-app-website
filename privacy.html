Privacy

Exactly what the app and this website do with your data.

Typing

  • Listen-only. It only notices that keys are pressed. It can’t change anything you type.
  • Classified, not kept. Each key is briefly classified on your Mac as a letter, word break or other character. Only counts and timing go to the detector, never your text.
  • No typing history. The app does not save, log or send your text. The input-monitoring code has no analytics. Stats record how much you typed, not what.
  • Password fields. Most password fields turn on a macOS feature called Secure Input, which hides your typing from Stop Typing App completely. Some apps don’t use it. There, your password is counted like any other typing, and the characters are never kept. To keep the app out of those apps entirely, add them to Ignore.
  • Your permission. It needs Input Monitoring, which you grant in System Settings and can take back any time.

What’s saved on your Mac

A nudge count and word estimate are saved locally. Old counts reset at midnight while the app runs, or when it next starts or wakes. Settings and your ignore list stay until you change or remove them.

Updates

The keyboard-monitoring app is sandboxed without network access. Update checks use Sparkle’s separate helpers to contact GitHub.

Automatic checks are off by default. If you turn them on or check manually, GitHub and its download servers receive your IP address, app and updater versions, and request language, never your typing. Sparkle’s helpers run outside the app sandbox and may keep local update logs, preferences and downloads. System profiling is disabled.

Notifications and system records

Optional notifications contain only generic reminders, but macOS keeps delivery times and may produce its own diagnostics.

This website

This website (not the app) uses Vercel Web Analytics and Speed Insights for cookie-free, aggregate visit and page-speed stats. The app itself has no analytics or tracking.

No third-party font service is loaded; fonts and images are served with the page. Our hosting provider still receives ordinary web requests, including your IP address. Downloads come straight from GitHub.

Open source

All of the source code is on GitHub, so you can read every line of the app’s code and see exactly what it does.
